## Recurring patterns across all 50 records

1. **Summary must lead to evidence, not replace it.** Every record starts with an orienting state, preserves a visible intermediate selection or detail, and ends with locally retained completion evidence.
2. **Selection continuity matters.** Whether the selected unit is a log event, issue, test attempt, finding, request, span, stack frame, incident update, or chart mark, later states are understandable only when identity and scope survive the transition.
3. **Investigation is reversible.** All 50 motion records can be paused, resumed, and revisited; their five local state images provide a discrete inspection path when animation is inappropriate.
4. **Failure is a state, not disappearance.** The common observed interruption is a paused or skipped evidence transition. Recovery resumes or seeks backward without changing provenance or pretending the omitted evidence was seen.
5. **Completion requires a specific state.** Each record names `media/state-05.png` and the corresponding motion asset as completion evidence instead of treating an aggregate, claim, or marketing frame as proof.
6. **Evidence identity outlives presentation.** Hashes, dimensions, duration, frame count, source URL, owner, and capture method remain stable even when viewers navigate backward or choose a different frame.

## Important disagreements

- **Query-first vs item-first:** observability and analytics products often begin with source, scope, and time; test, security, and incident products more often begin with a pre-existing run, finding, or incident identity.
- **Continuous time vs discrete hierarchy:** traces, profiles, performance recordings, and incident timelines use temporal navigation; CI/test reports, vulnerability findings, and audit tools emphasize nested rows, attempts, rules, and attachments.
- **Inline inspector vs page transition:** some products preserve the summary beside a detail panel, while others navigate to a dedicated evidence page. The records do not imply that one model fits every evidence density.
- **Mutable operational state vs sealed artifact:** incidents and live observability views can change while open; HTML reports, audit results, and retained recordings behave more like sealed evidence. Freshness and provenance requirements therefore differ.
- **Built-in motion vs recorded interaction:** products expose different animation surfaces. The local evidence contract normalizes only playback and retained states; it does not claim identical product-native timing, easing, or reduced-motion support.

## Applicability boundaries

- Use the summary-to-detail patterns when an aggregate can be traced to contributing records. Do not use them to imply causality that the source product does not show.
- Use synchronized time navigation for traces, performance, replay, and incident evidence; avoid it for unordered findings unless time is genuinely meaningful.
- Preserve retry or attempt identity in CI and test evidence. Never merge attachments or errors from different attempts into one apparently coherent run.
- Preserve severity, rule, resource, and remediation provenance in security and accessibility findings. A count without the underlying affected items is not completion evidence.
- Preserve query, data source, timezone, and freshness in observability and analytics views. A chart image alone is insufficient for a reproducible investigation.
- Treat the recordings as visual evidence only. The per-record accessibility unknowns explicitly prevent inferring screen-reader output, focus order, or product reduced-motion behavior from pixels.

## Shared first-success journey

1. Open the authentic local motion and establish product/evidence identity.
2. Start playback and orient to the summary or primary evidence surface.
3. Pause at a selected detail so the intermediate state can be inspected.
4. Resume or seek to deeper evidence while retaining source identity.
5. Reach the final retained state and cite it together with the hash-verified motion.

The shared journey is an indexing device, not a claim that all products use identical labels or controls. Product-specific names, source URLs, state hashes, failures, recoveries, and completion evidence live in the 50 linked records.

## Motion and recovery synthesis

Across the records, play starts continuous authentic motion; pause interrupts without invalidating evidence; seek supports reversal and comparison; resume provides the observed recovery. The five local states are the nonanimated alternative for this catalog. Exact product-native easing, transition duration, interruption semantics, and reduced-motion settings remain bounded by each record's observable source.

## Accessibility synthesis

The catalog can prove that a reviewer can inspect five discrete visual states without playing motion and that playback is pauseable/revisitable. It cannot prove product accessibility trees, screen-reader announcements, keyboard focus order, captions, or reduced-motion settings. Those unknowns are retained in every `reference.json` rather than inferred.

## Adoption rule

Adopt evidence identity, reversible investigation, explicit intermediate state, failure/recovery visibility, and completion proof. Avoid summaries without contributing records, hidden scope changes, mixed retries, unlabelled live/stale data, and decisions whose supporting artifact cannot be opened offline.
